YouTube Music Skips Songs by Itself in Chrome or Firefox: What to Check

Last reviewed 7 November 2026 · Chrome, Edge and Firefox on desktop

A song starts, plays for a few seconds, and YouTube Music skips to the next one by itself, sometimes several times in a row. Before changing anything, notice one detail: does it happen only while the tab is in the background, or also while you are looking at it? The answer tells you where to look.

First: background only, or always?

If songs skip only when the YouTube Music tab is in the background, start with the guide on YouTube Music stopping in a background tab: check browser tab-saving settings alongside connection problems and extensions. Background-only timing does not establish the cause. If it also happens while the tab is in front, work through the checklist below, one step at a time, and test after each.

Checklist

  1. Test without extensions. YouTube Help suggests checking “whether your browser extensions that block ads are affecting video playback”, or opening YouTube in an incognito window with all extensions disabled. YouTube Help YouTube also says that ad-blocking extensions “may affect video playback”. YouTube Help
    • Chrome: select More > Extensions > Manage extensions and turn extensions off one by one. Chrome Help
    • Edge: select Extensions next to the address bar, then Manage extensions, and use the toggles. Microsoft Support
    • Firefox: restart in Troubleshoot Mode, which Mozilla says temporarily disables all extensions. If the problem stops there, an extension, theme or hardware acceleration is the likely cause. Mozilla Support
  2. Check your connection. YouTube Help recommends restarting your internet connection and running a speed test for playback problems. YouTube Help YouTube Music Help asks for a strong data or Wi-Fi connection. YouTube Music Help
  3. Play on one device or browser at a time. YouTube Help says each Premium, Premium Lite or Music Premium member can stream on one device at a time; above that limit, playback “may be paused or not advance to the next video or track”. Chrome and Firefox on the same computer count as two streams. YouTube Help
  4. Clear cookies and site data for YouTube only. YouTube Help lists clearing your browser’s cache and cookies as a playback fix. YouTube Help You can do it for a single site instead of everything; you will need to sign in again afterwards:
    • Chrome: Settings > Privacy and security > Third-party cookies > See all site data and permissions, search for youtube, then Delete. Chrome Help
    • Edge: Settings > Privacy, search, and services > Cookies > See all cookies and site data, search for the site and delete it (menu names vary between versions). Microsoft Support
    • Firefox: on the YouTube Music tab, click the padlock or shield icon left of the address bar and choose Clear cookies and site data. Mozilla Support
  5. Check whether specific songs are unavailable. YouTube Help explains that some music can become unavailable in certain regions while licensing terms are being agreed, and becomes available again automatically afterwards. Licensing restrictions are one possible reason a particular track is unavailable; repeated failure alone does not establish the cause. YouTube Help
  6. Update your browser. Chrome, Edge, Firefox.

FAQ

Can an ad blocker make YouTube Music skip songs?

YouTube Help says browser extensions that block ads may affect video playback, and suggests testing in an incognito window with all extensions disabled. If skipping stops there, turn your extensions back on one by one to find the cause.

Songs only skip when the tab is in the background. What does that mean?

Start with the browser’s tab-saving settings, but timing alone does not prove the cause. See our guide on YouTube Music stopping in a background tab for checks in Chrome, Edge and Firefox.

Why does playback stop advancing when YouTube is open on another device?

YouTube Help says each Premium, Premium Lite or Music Premium member can stream on one device at a time. Above that limit, playback may be paused or may not advance to the next video or track. Different browsers on the same device count as separate streams.
